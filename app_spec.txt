    <overview>
        <paragraph>
            The application replaces an existing Google Sheets-based personal/family finance system
            (source file: Control_de_Ingresos_y_Gastos.xlsx). The spreadsheet tracks, on a monthly basis:
            income, expenses, bank/cash account balances, investments, credit card and loan debts,
            accounts receivable, and fixed assets, and produces a consolidated balance sheet
            (Assets / Liabilities / Equity) per month.
        </paragraph>
        <paragraph>
            The finances tracked span three currencies (CAD, USD, COP) and at least two countries
            (Canada and Colombia), evidenced by the accounts present in the source data — RBC, CIBC,
            TD Bank, and Wise on the Canadian/international side; Bancolombia, Davivienda, Nequi, and
            Éxito on the Colombian side — and by Colombia-specific concepts such as PILA (mandatory
            social security contributions).
        </paragraph>
        <paragraph>
            A core mechanic of the existing system is a monthly reconciliation: for every tracked
            account/liability, a "recorded" balance (read off a real bank/card statement) is compared
            against a "computed" balance (derived from the transaction ledger), and the difference is
            expected to be zero. The spreadsheet also computes two related expense totals to correct for
            credit-card/loan payments being logged as expenses alongside the purchases they later settle;
            these were historically named "Gasto Total" and "Gasto Real", and are renamed in this
            specification to "Total Recorded Disbursements" and "Net Operating Expenses" respectively
            (see business_rules).
        </paragraph>
    </overview>

    <goals>
        <goal>
            Replace hand-maintained, per-month spreadsheet formulas — particularly the account-balance
            reconciliation, which today requires manually re-curating a list of specific transaction row
            references for every account, every month — with balances computed by a query
            (account + period), so the system does not silently break as new transactions are added.
        </goal>
        <goal>
            Eliminate the credit-card/loan double-counting problem structurally (debt principal payments
            should never be posted as an expense category in the first place) rather than correcting for
            it after the fact with a separate "real vs. total" calculation.
        </goal>
        <goal>
            Continue supporting ongoing monthly tracking of income, expenses, account balances,
            investments, debts, receivables, and fixed assets across CAD, USD, and COP.
        </goal>
    </goals>

    <users>
        <note>
            The source material does not explicitly describe user roles. The data implies a single
            primary user tracking household finances that include a spouse/partner ("Diana", who also
            appears as an income source under a baking-related activity) and daughters (an allowance
            category, "MESADA NIÑAS", exists for them). It is not stated whether other household members
            have their own access to the system. See open_questions.
        </note>
    </users>

        <accounts>
            <description>
                Represents every bank/cash/investment account (assets) and every credit card/loan
                (liabilities) tracked per month. In the source spreadsheet these appeared across four
                sections that all shared the same structural pattern: "Cuentas" (bank/cash accounts),
                "INVERSIÓN" (investments), "CREDITOS Y DEUDAS" (credit cards and loans), and
                "CTAS X COBRAR" (accounts receivable).
            </description>

            <behavior id="recorded_vs_computed_vs_variance">
                Each account/liability carries three parallel sets of values per period, each expressed in
                CAD, USD, and COP:
                - "Recorded": the actual balance, as read off the real bank/card statement, entered manually.
                - "Computed": an expected balance, derived by taking a known prior balance and adding/
                  subtracting the transactions that touched that account during the period.
                - "Variance": Recorded minus Computed. This is expected to be (approximately) zero and is
                  the core reconciliation signal for the account.
            </behavior>

            <technical_requirement id="computed_balance_must_be_queryable">
                In the original spreadsheet, each account's "Computed" balance formula was a hand-built,
                manually-curated list of specific transaction-row references for that account, rebuilt by
                hand every month. This does not scale and is fragile (it silently breaks if transactions
                are inserted, deleted, or reordered upstream). The new system's equivalent computation MUST
                be a query — e.g. sum of transaction amounts where account = X and period = Y — which
                requires each transaction to carry an explicit account reference. This mirrors the pattern
                already used correctly, in the source spreadsheet, for the per-category actuals report
                (see functional_requirements/category_actuals_report), which aggregates by category and
                period without any hardcoded row references.
            </technical_requirement>

            <example>
                <scenario>
                    Source spreadsheet, June 2026, "RBC Checking" account. Recorded balance: 3,674.22
                    (CAD). Original computed-balance formula, preserved verbatim to illustrate the pattern

                    "=3674.22-Gastos!G465-Gastos!G450-500-Gastos!G431-Gastos!G430+B6-Gastos!G421-700-Gastos!G420-300-Gastos!G415-Gastos!G411-Gastos!G397-100-Gastos!G396+B8-Gastos!G376+B7-Gastos!G363-Gastos!G362"
                </scenario>
                <expected_result>
                    Variance (Recorded minus Computed) should equal zero once all transactions for the
                    account and period are correctly recorded and categorized.
                </expected_result>
            </example>

        <exchange_rates>
            <description>
                Three currencies are used throughout: CAD, USD, COP. Monetary totals that mix currencies
                are folded into a single CAD-equivalent figure using period-specific exchange rates.
            </description>

            <data_requirement id="three_independent_rate_series">
                The system must store three independent exchange rate series, one value per accounting
                period for each of the following source-labeled pairs, and must NOT derive any one of them
                from the other two:
                - the rate labeled "USD / COP" in the source
                - the rate labeled "USD / CAD" in the source
                - the rate labeled "CAD" in the source (used in formulas as a COP-per-1-CAD figure)
                This is a deliberate structural requirement, not a limitation to work around: in the source
                data these three rates were confirmed to be independently, manually maintained, and are NOT
                mutually consistent with each other (see example below). Deriving one from the other two
                would silently change historical computed totals. Exact field/column naming for these rate
                series is not specified by the source material and is left to implementation.
            </data_requirement>

            <business_rule id="conversion_direction">
                For a rate labeled "USD / COP" with value X, 1 USD = X COP. For a rate labeled "USD / CAD"
                with value X, 1 USD = X CAD. To convert an amount FROM USD into the other currency,
                multiply the USD amount by the rate. Confirmed directly by the source owner. The third
                rate (labeled "CAD", used as a COP-per-1-CAD figure) follows the same
                divide-toward-the-target-unit logic used in the source formulas: dividing a COP amount by
                this rate yields its CAD equivalent.
            </business_rule>

            <example>
                <scenario>
                    Across all 21 in-scope months (January 2025 through September 2026), the "USD/COP"
                    rate stayed fixed at 4,400 and the "USD/CAD" rate stayed fixed at 0.75 in every single
                    month, while the "CAD" (CAD-to-COP) rate changed over the same period: 3,000
                    (early 2025) to 2,550 (mid-2026) to 2,200 (September 2026). Cross-checking: if the
                    CAD-to-COP rate were derived from the other two, it would compute to
                    4,400 / 0.75 = 5,866.67, which does not match the actual recorded values in any month
                    (2,200-3,000). This confirms the three rates are independently maintained in the source
                    data, not mutually derived.
                </scenario>
            </example>
        </exchange_rates>

    <business_rules>

        <business_rule id="total_recorded_disbursements">
            <name_es>Gasto Total</name_es>
            <name_en>Total Recorded Disbursements</name_en>
            <definition>
                The sum of every expense category's total for the month, across every category code,
                including categories that represent credit-card/loan payments (C009, C010, C027, C038,
                C039, C044, C046) rather than real spending.
            </definition>
        </business_rule>

        <business_rule id="net_operating_expenses">
            <name_es>Gasto Real / Gastos Real</name_es>
            <name_en>Net Operating Expenses</name_en>
            <definition>
                Total Recorded Disbursements, MINUS the PRINCIPAL component of any debt/credit-card
                payments included in it, MINUS depreciation. INTEREST components of debt payments ARE
                included in Net Operating Expenses — confirmed directly by the source owner.
            </definition>
            <rationale>
                Paying down a credit card or a loan is not, itself, a new expense — the underlying purchase
                was already recorded as an expense at the time it was made. Counting both the original
                purchase and the later bill payment as expenses double-counts the same spending.
            </rationale>
        </business_rule>

        <business_rule id="debt_principal_not_expense">
            <definition>
                A loan or credit-card PRINCIPAL repayment is not an expense; it is a balance-sheet
                transaction that reduces a liability, funded by reducing an asset (cash). Only the INTEREST
                portion of a debt payment is a real expense and should be included in Net Operating
                Expenses. Confirmed by the source owner: interest counts toward Net Operating Expenses;
                only principal is excluded.
            </definition>
            <technical_requirement>
                Principal and interest must be distinguishable at the data level (not only in free-text
                memo text) for every debt category (C009, C010, C027, C038, C039, C044, C046), via the
                "debt_component" field on transactions (see data_requirements/transactions). For the
                already-extracted 2025-2026 historical data, the existing memo text already distinguishes
                these for every sampled case (e.g. "FORD ESC CAPITAL" vs. "FORD ESC INTERESES"), so tagging
                historical rows during import should be mechanical rather than requiring manual re-entry.
            </technical_requirement>
        </business_rule>

    </business_rules>

    <functional_requirements>

        <reconciliation>
            <behavior id="monthly_reconciliation_check">
                <name_es>Resultado Mes</name_es>
                <description>
                    Each month, the system must verify that recorded account/liability balances agree with
                    what the transaction ledger implies they should be. In the source spreadsheet this was
                    a single formula combining income, the net variance across every account/investment/
                    debt/receivable section, Total Recorded Disbursements, that month's debt-payment
                    total, and that month's credit-card-charges total, with hand-picked signs, expected to
                    evaluate to (approximately) zero.
                </description>
                <example>
                    <scenario>
                        Source formula (June 2026), preserved verbatim for reference:
                        "=B13+(K25+L25*G4+M25/J3)-(G8+I9/J3)-(K30+L30*G4+M30/J3)+(K41+L41*G4+M41/J3)-(K50+L50*G4+M50/J3)-K10+K6*2"
                    </scenario>
                </example>
                <technical_requirement>
                    This single hand-assembled formula should NOT be ported verbatim. The equivalent
                    correctness check in the new system should be a standard trial-balance / accounting-
                    equation check, computed independently from the ledger and account balances
                    (Assets = Liabilities + Equity, with the residual expected to be at or near zero),
                    rather than a manually-maintained formula with hand-picked signed terms.
                </technical_requirement>
            </behavior>
        </reconciliation>

        <category_actuals_report>
            <requirement>
                For each category and period, the system must be able to report the actual amount spent
                (summed across CAD/USD/COP and folded into one currency-equivalent figure) versus the
                manually maintained monthly budget figure for that category (see
                data_requirements/budget), and the resulting over/under-budget ratio. In the source
                spreadsheet, this was the one part of the system that queried the ledger correctly (by
                category and period, without hardcoded row references), and should be used as the model
                for how account balances are computed too (see data_requirements/accounts/
                technical_requirement).
